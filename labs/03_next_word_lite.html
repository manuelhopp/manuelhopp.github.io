Your task: build two continuations of the sentence yourself, token by token, and see where each one ends.

In Video 2, GPT-2 continued "Working memory can hold only about …" with "1.5 billion bytes". Now you choose. In the box above, click three tokens one after another, then click Let the model finish. Then click Start again and try a second path.

Predict first: can you find a path that ends in a correct statement, such as "about four meaningful units"?

Compare with the model answer

You can build 125 paths here (five choices at each of three steps). None of them says "about four" or mentions chunks, and 96 of them end with computer words such as bytes, bits, data or files. The correct answer was never on offer: four is not among the eight most likely first tokens. Research on human working memory points to about four meaningful units (Cowan, 2001). A plausible reason for the computer words is that "memory" often appears in texts about computers. The labs do not measure this.

A model produces text that is likely, not text that is checked. When likely text is false, it still sounds just as fluent.

About the data. All chances on this page were calculated once with the open model GPT-2 (small: 124 million parameters, the numbers that are set during training, far fewer than in current chat models; Radford et al., 2019) and stored in this page. The model saw the text only up to the gap. For three steps you can pick among the five most likely candidates (5 × 5 × 5 = 125 paths); after that, or whenever you click Let the model finish, the page shows the model's most likely continuation (14 tokens). Values are rounded. A modern chat model would give different numbers.
