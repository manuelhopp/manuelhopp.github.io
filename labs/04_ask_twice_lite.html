1 Task 1 · Ten times the same question

Your task: let the model write the same continuation ten times, at three temperatures, and count the different answers.

Video 3 used a different starting text. Here the model continues "A new study on homework found that …". No study is named, so there is nothing true to report.

Predict: the slider starts at 0.7. If you ask for the same continuation 10 times, how many different "findings" will you get? Click Generate. Then try temperature 0 and 1.5.

Compare with the model answer

At 0.7 you get 10 different "findings" out of 10, although every token was drawn from the same chances. At 0 you get the same sentence 10 times: the most likely token is taken at every step. At 1.5 all 10 differ, and most stop making sense after a few words.

2 Task 2 · Same answer, so true?

Your task: decide whether an answer that comes out the same ten times is a finding you could cite.

At temperature 0 the model wrote ten times: "A new study on homework found that students who took the test were more likely to take the test than those who did not."

Is this a finding you could cite? And what would you do before you use any answer of a chatbot for your studies?

Compare with the model answer

No. No study stands behind it: the starting text named none, and the model produced the most likely continuation, ten times the same. The sentence is even circular. A repeated answer is consistent, not correct.

For research use: run important tasks more than once and compare; check a sample yourself; write down the tool, version, date and settings. A low temperature makes answers repeatable, not true.

Before you go on

Take-away
Temperature controls how much answers vary, not whether they are true.

About the data. All answers on this page were produced once with the open model GPT-2 (small; Radford et al., 2019) and stored in this page: 10 answers of 20 tokens for each setting, with a fixed random seed. The bars show the model's real chances for the first token after the starting text. Answers were screened for offensive words. A modern chat model may give different numbers and a different range; the way the tokens are drawn is the same.
